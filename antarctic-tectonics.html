Research

Antarctic Tectonics

Magmatism and metamorphism along the Ross Orogen in the Transantarctic Mountains.

How does a continental arc grow and evolve, how does it end, and what happens to the Gondwana margin afterwards? We use the Transantarctic Mountains to test whether arc magmatism builds new crust or recycles old crust, and we follow the same margin forward through the Permian, Jurassic and Cretaceous.

The Ross Orogen: a long-lived continental arc

Our working hypothesis is that the Ross arc was long-lived and compositionally uniform, with alkaline and subalkaline magmas emplaced together, and that its end came through foundering of the deep crust.

  • The Granite Harbour Intrusives of Northern Victoria Land record a long-lived, compositionally homogeneous arc. Cottle and Erickson 2026, Lithos
  • Alkaline and subalkaline magmas were emplaced at the same time in the same arc, and the Ross record shows how much new crust the arc added compared with recycled crust. Hagen-Peter and Cottle 2016, Lithos; Hagen-Peter and Cottle 2017, Gondwana Research
  • In the Dry Valleys, a short-lived subduction-related magma system mixed lithospheric mantle with crustal components, and coupled garnet Lu-Hf and monazite U-Pb dates constrain the early dynamics of the convergent margin. Hagen-Peter et al. 2015, GSA Lithosphere; Hagen-Peter, Cottle and Smit 2016, Journal of Metamorphic Geology
  • The earliest Ross magmatism in southern Victoria Land includes a calc-alkaline gabbro and an A-type granite. Cottle and Cooper 2006, New Zealand Journal of Geology and Geophysics (two papers)
  • The lower crust beneath the Transantarctic Mountains has its own thermal history, recorded in U-Pb thermochronology of xenoliths. Apen and Cottle 2023, Chemical Geology

After the Ross: Permian to Jurassic magmatism

The Gondwana margin kept producing magma long after the Ross orogeny ended. Demian Nelson's work traces that younger history across Antarctica.

  • Permian volcanism of the Choiyoi Province, known from South America, extends into central Antarctica. Nelson and Cottle 2019, GSA Lithosphere
  • Butcher Ridge in the Ferrar large igneous province is a glassy, layered silicic magma distribution center of Jurassic age, and its hydrated glass preserves ultra-depleted hydrogen isotopes that record Late Cretaceous glaciation in Antarctica. Nelson, Cottle and Schoene 2019, GSA Bulletin; Nelson, Cottle, Bindeman and Camacho 2022, Nature Communications
  • Zircon U-Pb and Hf data link the Gondwana arc record of West Antarctica, Australia and South America, and show that the paleo-Pacific margin was segmented through time. Nelson and Cottle 2017, Tectonics; Nelson and Cottle 2018, Gondwana Research
  • The same margin ended in New Zealand with prolonged melting and garnet growth during the separation of Zealandia from Gondwana. Briggs and Cottle 2018, GSA Lithosphere; Briggs, Cottle and Smit 2018, Journal of Metamorphic Geology
