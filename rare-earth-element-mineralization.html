Research

Rare Earth Element Mineralization

Petrochronology of the rocks that host rare earth deposits.

Where and when do rare earth and other critical-mineral deposits form? We date the magmatic and hydrothermal events behind mineralization, so that the ages of ore-forming events and of their host rocks can be compared directly.

Rare earth elements in California

Phosphate minerals such as monazite and xenotime are the main source of the rare earth elements, and the California deposits are among the few in the United States with high concentrations.

  • At Mountain Pass, titanite and zircon U-Pb dates from the ultrapotassic intrusions overlap monazite Th-Pb dates from the carbonatite, which links the two suites in time. Poletti, Cottle and Hagen-Peter 2016, Journal of Petrology
  • In Music Valley, rare earth phosphates formed by identifiable mechanisms in Proterozoic gneiss. McKinney, Cottle and Lederer 2015, GSA Bulletin

Critical minerals in other settings

The same dating approach applies to tin, copper and gold systems in volcanic and magmatic belts.

  • Zircon and cassiterite U-Pb dates constrain early Tertiary tin mineralization at Mayo Darlé, Cameroon, and Cenozoic felsic volcanics in Cameroon show where epithermal gold may form. Tantoh et al. 2023, Journal of Geochemical Exploration; Fai et al. 2027, Journal of African Earth Sciences
  • Zircon chemistry and ages identify porphyry copper systems in Iran and northeast India, and flag the magmas that were fertile. Raeisi et al. 2023, Journal of Geochemical Exploration; Majumdar et al. 2024, International Journal of Earth Sciences
  • Zircon chemistry and ages show where uranium-rich granitoids occur in southwest Cameroon. Embui et al. 2019, Acta Geochimica

Reading minerals as records of process

Dating a deposit requires knowing how the dated mineral formed.

  • Monazite trace elements and isotopes record the conditions of metamorphism and the assembly of plutons. Holder et al. 2015, Chemical Geology; Cottle, Lederer and Larson 2019, Chemical Geology; Larson et al. 2021, Geoscience Frontiers
  • A new collaborative project with Robert Holder at the University of Michigan tests whether partial melting of metasedimentary rocks differentiates the heat-producing elements in continental crust. NSF 2537249

Newer work extends these studies to Colorado, Nevada and Tanzania.
