Research

New techniques in geochronology

Time-of-flight imaging, laser ablation and mass spectrometry for in situ dating of accessory minerals.

We develop and apply laser ablation methods that date minerals and measure their chemistry at the same time, so that a date can be tied directly to the metamorphic, magmatic or hydrothermal process that produced it. Alongside U-Th-Pb, we date minerals by in situ Rb-Sr and Lu-Hf, which extends dating to micas, feldspars, garnet, apatite and other phases that lack U. Our newest capability is time-of-flight mass spectrometry, which records the whole mass spectrum from every laser pulse.

LA-ICP-ToF-MS imaging

The UCSB laboratory couples a Nu Instruments Vitesse ICP-ToF-MS to an ESL 193 nm ArF excimer laser ablation system fitted with a TwoVol3 (TV3) fast-washout cell. The combination turns each laser pulse into a short burst of ions, and the analyser records every element in that burst, so adding elements to the measurement costs no time.

  • m/z 23 to 238sodium through uranium in every pixel
  • 1000 Hzlaser repetition rate for imaging, with 5 µm lines
  • About 90×faster than conventional spot analysis
  • About 1 msaerosol washout, so single pulses stay distinct

What it makes possible

  • Dates and chemistry from the same volume. U-Pb and Th-Pb dates, together with Ti, rare earth elements, Th/U and Lu/Hf, come from the same pixels, which is what petrochronology needs.
  • Age maps of single grains and whole populations. Hundreds of zircon, monazite, titanite or allanite grains can be imaged in the time a few dozen spots used to take, and pixels are pooled into domains defined by chemistry, cathodoluminescence or age.
  • Multi-element mapping. Trace element maps of sulfides, oxides, silicates and ore minerals support work on zoning, mineralization and fluid history.
  • Single particle and aerosol analysis. The full spectrum is recorded for each particle, which opens applications in environmental, archaeological and health-related geochemistry.
  • A permanent spatial archive. Because the whole spectrum is stored, a map can be re-examined later for elements nobody planned to measure.

Secondary reference zircons from the 3.45 Ga OG1 to the 336 Ma Plešovice reproduce concordant dates from half-second lines, and the results stay reproducible across days.

Instruments and lasers at UCSB

  • Nu Instruments Vitesse ICP-ToF-MS. Reflectron time-of-flight analyser sampled at up to 200 kHz, with a quadrupole pre-filter, an always-on reaction cell and a beam attenuation system that extends dynamic range.
  • Nu-Plasma HR. Multicollector ICP-MS with an enhanced-sensitivity interface, full and pseudo-high mass resolution, and a fixed collector array with zoom optics.
  • Nu-Plasma P3D. Multicollector ICP-MS equipped with six Daly detectors for sensitive, high-precision isotope ratio measurements.
  • Agilent 8900 triple quadrupole ICP-MS. A reaction cell instrument used for in situ Rb-Sr and Lu-Hf geochronology, where the cell separates isobaric interferences such as 87Rb on 87Sr and 176Lu and 176Yb on 176Hf, and for sensitive trace element analysis.
  • Two ESL 193 nm ArF excimer lasers. Each system runs at up to 1 kHz and is equipped with a TwoVol3 (TV3) fast-washout cell, which suits rapid imaging and single-pulse analysis, and either laser can be coupled to the ICP instruments above.

Together these support rapid, spatially resolved imaging of thin sections and grain mounts as well as targeted spot analysis. More information on the analytical facilities is available at the UCSB ICP laboratory site and the Laser Ablation Split Stream Laboratory.

GSA 2026 short course

We taught Fundamentals and Geoscience Applications of Time-of-Flight Mass Spectrometry (SC26CN05) at GSA Connects 2026 in Denver on 10 October, with Andrew Kylander-Clark and Morgan Adamson. The day covered ToF physics, sample introduction and laser ablation, acquisition in practice, case studies in petrochronology, ore systems and single particle analysis, a live session on the UCSB instrument, and a hands-on data processing workshop. The course was supported by Elemental Scientific Lasers and Nu Instruments.

Reference materials for monazite and allanite

The main limitation on accurate standard-based 208Pb/232Th ages from monazite and allanite is the lack of well-characterized reference materials. Both minerals vary in composition and show matrix-dependent fractionation during analysis, so standards must be closely matched to unknowns, yet Th-Pb ages exist for only one commonly used reference material, known as 554. We determined isotope dilution Th-Pb ages for a suite of monazite and allanite reference materials, which allows Th-Pb ages to be calibrated independently of U-Pb and linked to tectonic processes.
